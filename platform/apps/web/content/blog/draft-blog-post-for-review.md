## The search behind the search

If you typed "ai marketing agency for startups" into a box this week, you're not shopping for software. You're doing the math on a hire.

You've got a product, a little traction, and a marketing to-do list that's longer than your runway. The options all have a catch. An agency wants $4–8k/month and a three-month ramp. A full-time marketer is $90k+ and one person's bandwidth. And the "AI marketing tools" everyone keeps recommending? Most of them hand you a dashboard and a blank cursor — you still do the work, now with extra tabs.

So let's answer the real question: what should an AI marketing agency for a startup actually *do* for you — and how do you tell the ones that ship from the ones that just suggest?

## What "agency" should mean (and usually doesn't)

An agency is supposed to be a *team*. Strategy, research, copy, ads, outreach — different jobs, different specialists, one coordinated push.

Most AI marketing products quietly drop the "team" part. You get one general-purpose chat box that's good at first drafts and useless at follow-through. It won't notice your competitor changed their pricing page. It won't keep your blog calendar warm while you're shipping. It definitely won't run an ad campaign end to end.

The bar to look for: **specialists, not a single generalist.** At ipop, that's the department — Scout on SEO and research, Lens on brand, Quill on content, plus agents for ads and outbound. Each one owns a lane and works it around the clock. You steer; they ship. (You can [meet the whole department](https://ipop.ai/) on the homepage — they're a chatty bunch.)
