# AI Marketing Agency vs. Hiring a Marketer: An Honest Cost Breakdown for Founders

You've got a product, a little traction, and a nagging feeling that marketing is the thing you keep meaning to get to. So you're weighing your options: hire someone, find a freelancer, sign with an agency, or hand it to AI.

We're an AI marketing department, so you already know which way we lean. But you came here for a real comparison, not a sales pitch wearing a blog post's clothes. So here's the honest version — what each option actually costs, how fast it moves, and where it bites you.

## The four options, plainly

**A first marketing hire.** A full-time generalist who owns it all. Powerful, but it's a real commitment — salary, ramp time, and the gamble that you hired the right person before you knew exactly what you needed.

**A freelancer or contractor.** Cheaper, faster to start, no long-term commitment. The catch: they're usually one specialist (a writer *or* an ads person *or* an SEO person), and they're juggling other clients.

**A traditional agency.** A whole team on paper. But you're often paying retainer rates for junior execution, and the people in the pitch meeting aren't always the people doing the work.

**An AI marketing department.** A fleet of specialist agents — research, brand, content, ads, outbound — that draft and plan around the clock. You steer; they ship. (That's us. More on the honest limits below.)

## The cost, without the hand-waving

Here's where most comparisons get vague. Let's use rough, real numbers — adjust for your market.

| Option | Typical monthly cost | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| First marketing hire | $6,000–$12,000+ (salary ÷ 12, before benefits) | One person, full-time, learning your product |
| Experienced freelancer | $3,000–$8,000 | One specialist, part of their attention |
| Traditional agency | $4,000–$15,000 retainer | A team, often junior execution |
| AI marketing department | Free to start, then a flat plan | A full specialist roster, around the clock |

A few honest caveats on that table:

- The hire number ignores recruiting time, the months before they're productive, and what it costs if it's the wrong fit.
- Freelancer and agency ranges swing wildly by market and seniority — treat these as a starting point, not gospel.
- "Free to start" is literally how ours works: you can [start with no card](/start) and pick a plan when the work pays for itself. The full breakdown lives on our [pricing page](/pricing).

The point isn't that cheaper wins. It's that the sticker price is the smallest part of the decision.
