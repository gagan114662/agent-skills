**Target:** `AI marketing agency for startups`

Why this one over the alternatives I weighed:

- **It's bottom-funnel.** Someone typing "AI marketing agency" *plus* "for startups" isn't browsing — they're sizing up a hire. That's buyer intent, not curiosity.
- **It matches what we actually are.** Our own homepage leads with "The marketing agency of AI agents — you steer, they ship." The search and the page agree, so the post won't overpromise.
- **The persona fits the buyer.** Workspace positioning says the buyer is "a founder or small team who wants real marketing work shipped, not another dashboard." "For startups" is that person.
- **Rejected:** `AI marketing tools` (too top-funnel, tool-shopping not agency-shopping), `best AI marketing software` (listicle bait, we'd be one of ten), `marketing automation` (different mental model — pipelines, not people).

Looked at: `ipop.ai/` (hero, "Meet the department," "How it works"), `ipop.ai/pricing` (Starter/Pro/Agency, "Start free — no card").

# AI marketing agency for startups: what you actually get

You're a founder. Marketing is the thing that keeps sliding to next week. You don't want another dashboard to log into and feel guilty about — you want the work *done*. SEO researched, posts drafted, ads planned, outreach written.

So you search "AI marketing agency for startups," and you get a wall of tools that mostly hand you a blank text box and wish you luck.

We built ipop because the blank text box was the problem. Here's what an AI marketing agency should actually look like when you're a team of one to ten.

## It's a department, not a tool

A tool waits for you to use it. A department shows up and does the job.

ipop is a fleet of specialist agents, each with a lane:

- **Scout** runs SEO — keyword research, what's ranking, where you have a shot.
- **Quill** writes — blog drafts, landing copy, the stuff you keep meaning to publish. (Hi. That's us.)
- **Echo** handles brand voice, so everything sounds like you and not like a robot reading a thesaurus.
- Plus the rest of the bench for ads and outbound.

They draft, research, and plan around the clock. You steer; they ship. Made by robots, steered by humans — which brings us to the part founders actually care about.

## You approve anything that costs money or can't be undone

The fear with handing marketing to AI is waking up to a $4,000 ad spend and an email blast you never saw.

That doesn't happen here, by design. Every irreversible or money action is held for your approval. The agents do the heavy lifting — the research, the drafts, the plans — and then they stop and wait for you on anything that leaves the building or touches your card. You get the speed of automation with a human hand on the brake.

## What the first week looks like

Not "set up your integrations and configure your workspace." More like:

1. Tell us what you're selling and to whom.
2. Scout comes back with keywords you can realistically rank for.
3. Quill drafts the posts and pages to go after them.
4. Echo makes sure it all sounds like you.
5. You review, you tweak, you approve. It ships.

Real output you can read, not a settings screen.

## "Can a startup afford this?"

You start free — no card. You pick a plan when the work has already paid for itself, and you change or cancel any time. The tiers (Starter, Pro, Agency) scale with how much you want the team to take on, but the on-ramp is the same for everyone: try it, see the drafts, *then* decide.
