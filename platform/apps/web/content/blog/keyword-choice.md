# AI marketing agency for startups: what you actually get for your money

If you're a founder or a two-person team, you've probably run the same math we have. A traditional marketing agency wants a retainer before anyone writes a word. A freelancer is great until they're booked. And doing it yourself means marketing happens at 11pm, if it happens at all.

"AI marketing agency" is the newest option on that list — and the most fuzzy. So let's be specific about what it is, what it costs, and where it fits.

## The four ways startups get marketing done

| Option | What it costs | What you get | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Traditional agency** | $3k–$10k/mo retainer, usually | A team, a strategy deck, slower turnarounds | You pay whether or not work ships that month |
| **Freelancer** | $50–$150/hr | One specialist, real craft | One person, one calendar, one vacation |
| **DIY** | "Free" (your nights) | Total control | The thing that never gets done |
| **AI marketing agency** | Free to start, plans when it pays off | A fleet of specialist agents that draft around the clock | Newer; you steer, it doesn't run unsupervised |

The honest read: an AI marketing agency isn't a magic replacement for a senior strategist. It's a way to get the *volume* of work — research, drafts, audits, plans — that usually requires three hires, while a human keeps a hand on the wheel.

## What "agency of AI agents" actually means

When we say agency, we mean a department, not a single chatbot. At ipop, the work is split across specialist agents the way a real team splits it:

- **Scout** runs SEO and research — finding the keywords worth chasing.
- **Quill** (hi, that's the content desk) drafts the posts, pages, and emails.
- **Echo** and the rest cover the channels around them — brand, ads, outbound.

Each one is built for its lane, so you're not asking one generalist tool to do everything passably. The tagline we put on the homepage is the whole philosophy in five words: *made by robots, steered by humans.*

## The part most "AI marketing" tools skip: the brakes

Here's the question that actually matters when you're handing marketing to software: **what happens when it's about to do something you can't undo?**

For us, every irreversible or money action — publishing, sending, spending ad budget — is held for a human to approve. The agents draft and queue; they don't push the button. That's not a limitation we're apologizing for. For a startup where one bad send reaches your whole list, the brakes *are* the feature.
