# AI marketing agency vs. hiring a marketer: the honest math for a small team

You've got one budget line and a decision to make: your first marketing hire, a freelancer, an agency on retainer — or one of these AI marketing departments everyone's suddenly selling. We make one of those (hi, we're ipop), so treat us as a biased narrator. We'll still give you the version where we lose, because you'll figure it out anyway.

Here's the actual tradeoff, by the thing you're really buying.

## What you're paying for with a human marketer

A good marketer is judgment plus hands. They'll read your market, pick the bets, and do the work. The catch for a small team is rarely the person — it's the shape of the cost:

- **One person, one skill set.** Your great brand hire isn't also your great paid-ads hire isn't also your SEO hire. You buy depth in one lane and hope.
- **Ramp time.** A first hire spends weeks learning your product before the first thing ships. [CLAIM CHECK: typical ramp for a first marketing hire — leave qualitative or source it.]
- **Fixed cost, variable output.** Salary doesn't flex with how much marketing you actually need that month.

A retainer agency fixes the breadth problem and trades it for a queue: you're one of many accounts, and "we'll get to it" is a real answer.

## What you're paying for with an AI marketing department

Different shape. Instead of one person across one lane, you get a fleet of specialists — in our case Scout on SEO, Quill on content, Echo and the rest — drafting, researching, and planning around the clock. The pitch on our homepage is "you steer, they ship," and that's the honest division of labor: **the agents do the volume, you keep the wheel.**

What that buys a small team:
- **Breadth without four hires.** SEO, content, ads, and outbound running in parallel instead of sequentially.
- **No ramp queue.** Drafts start the same day, not the same quarter.
