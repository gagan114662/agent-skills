# AI Marketing Agency for Startups: Do You Even Need One?

You're a founder. Marketing is the thing you keep meaning to do after the next sprint. So you start pricing out an agency — and the retainer quote lands somewhere between "a junior hire" and "are you serious."

Before you sign anything, let's lay the three options on the table and be honest about each. We make one of these, so read us with a healthy squint — we've tried to keep the receipts in here so you can.

## Option 1: A traditional marketing agency

**What you get:** A team of humans, a strategy deck, and a monthly retainer.

**What it costs:** Most boutique agencies start at $3k–$8k/month, and the good ones want a 3–6 month commitment before they'll show results. You're often paying for account managers, not just the work.

**Where it shines:** Big, ambiguous, high-stakes bets — a rebrand, a category launch, a campaign that needs a human who's done it twenty times.

**Where it pinches:** Speed and control. Your blog post is in someone's queue behind four other clients. Every small change is an email thread. And you rarely see *how* the work gets made — just the invoice.

## Option 2: Hire someone in-house

**What you get:** One person who owns marketing.

**What it costs:** A marketing generalist runs $70k–$120k/year fully loaded, plus the months it takes to hire and ramp.

**Where it shines:** Context. They live inside your company and learn your product.

**Where it pinches:** One person can't be your SEO lead *and* your copywriter *and* your ads buyer *and* your outbound rep. You hired a generalist and quietly need five specialists.
