## Keyword pick + why

**Target keyword:** `AI marketing agency for startups` (with the secondary intent phrase *"AI marketing agency vs hiring a marketer"* woven in).

**Why this one:** It's bottom-of-funnel. A founder typing this has already decided they need marketing help and is now comparing *how* to get it — DIY, a freelancer, an agency retainer, a first in-house hire, or an AI department. That's a buyer, not a browser. It also maps cleanly to what our own site already sells against: "Hire a whole marketing department of AI agents… you steer, they ship" (from the homepage meta) and "Start free — no card" (pricing page). I'm anchoring the draft to claims our site actually makes so we're not writing checks the product can't cash.

What I looked at: `ipop.ai/` (hero + "Meet the department"), `ipop.ai/pricing` (Starter / Pro / Agency, free start), and the workspace product framing (human approval on every money move). I did **not** invent prices — our crawled pricing page shows tier names and "start free," not dollar amounts, so the draft stays vague on cost on purpose.

You're a founder. It's 11pm. The product is good, but the only people who know that are you and the seven users you onboarded by hand. Let's lay out the actual options, no sales fog.

### Your five choices (be honest about which one you've been avoiding)

1. **Do it yourself.** Free, except for the part where it costs you the company. Every hour on a blog post is an hour not on the product.
2. **Hire a freelancer.** Good for one thing at a time. You become the project manager. The strategy is still yours to invent.
3. **Sign with a traditional agency.** Real expertise, real retainer. Great when you have budget and patience; rough when you have neither.
4. **Make your first marketing hire.** The right long-term move — eventually. A single generalist can't be your SEO lead, brand writer, and ads buyer at once, and the search starts at three months and a salary.
5. **Hire an AI marketing department.** The new option, and the one you came here to size up. So let's size it up honestly.

### "AI marketing agency vs hiring a marketer" — the comparison you actually came for

A first marketing hire is one person wearing five hats badly for a while, then one hat well. An AI marketing department is the five hats from day one — but you're the one steering.

At ipop, that department has names. **Scout** runs SEO and research. **Quill** writes. **Echo** handles the rest of the lineup alongside the ads and outbound specialists. They draft, research, and plan around the clock; you review and decide. The site's own line for it is *"you steer, they ship,"* and that's the part we want to be precise about, because it's the part most "AI marketing" pitches lie about.

It does not mean a bot quietly spends your ad budget at 3am.

Here's the rule the whole system is built on: **every irreversible or money move is held for a human.** The agents will draft the campaign, build the keyword plan, and write the email sequence.
