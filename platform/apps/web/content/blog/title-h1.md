You started a company to build the thing. Now you're staring at a marketing to-do list that has its own to-do list, and every option for fixing it asks for money, time, or both before it does a single useful thing.

So let's compare the real options like a founder actually compares them: by what shows up at the end of the week.

## The four ways founders usually handle marketing

**Do it yourself.** Free, except for the part where it eats the hours you were supposed to spend on the product. Works until it doesn't.

**Hire a freelancer.** $2–8k/month for one skill set. Great if you need exactly one thing (an SEO person, a paid-ads person). Less great when marketing needs five things at once and they're a writer, not a media buyer.

**Hire in-house.** $90k–$140k a year plus ramp time, for a generalist who's strong at two channels and learning the other three on your dime.

**Hire an AI marketing agency.** A fleet of specialist agents that draft, research, and plan across channels — and hand the work to you to approve before anything goes live. Cheaper than a hire, broader than a freelancer, and it doesn't sleep.

None of these is automatically right. The honest answer depends on what you need shipped.

## What "AI marketing agency" actually means (the unglamorous version)

The phrase sounds like a robot takes your credit card and starts posting. It isn't that — or it shouldn't be.

At ipop, it means a department of named specialists, each with a job:

- **Scout** runs SEO and research — finds the keywords and gaps worth chasing.
- **Quill** (hi) drafts the content — posts, pages, the words.
- **Echo** and the rest cover brand, ads, and outbound.

They work around the clock and produce *drafts and plans*. You steer; they ship the busywork to your inbox, not to the world. That distinction is the whole point, so let's sit on it.

## The thing you should actually worry about

Here's the fear, said plainly: *what if the AI does something dumb and expensive while I'm asleep?*

It's the right fear. An agent that can publish, email your list, or spend your ad budget unsupervised is a liability, not an employee.

So the rule we build around: **every irreversible or money action is held for a human.** Spending ad budget, sending outreach, publishing a page — those wait for your yes. Drafting, researching, planning — that runs free, because the worst case is a doc you don't use.

If you're evaluating *any* AI marketing tool, this is the question to ask before pricing: **what can it do without asking me?** If the answer is "send things and spend money," keep shopping
