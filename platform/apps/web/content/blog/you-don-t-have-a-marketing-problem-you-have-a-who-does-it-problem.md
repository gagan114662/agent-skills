## You don't have a marketing problem. You have a "who does it" problem.

The work is clear enough: someone needs to research keywords, write the posts, watch the brand, run the ads, send the outbound. The hard part is deciding *who*. For most founders that's a three-way fork, and the options are usually framed by whoever's selling you one of them.

So here's the fork, framed by nobody's quota.

## Option 1: Hire a marketer

**What you get:** one human, full-time, who owns it.
**What it costs:** a salary. In the US, a mid-level generalist runs well into six figures, plus the months it takes to hire and the ramp before they ship anything.
**Where it bites:** one person can't be great at SEO *and* brand *and* paid *and* copy *and* outbound. You hired a generalist, so you get generalist depth across the board. And if they leave, the knowledge leaves with them.

Right call when: you have the budget and the volume to keep one person genuinely busy and growing.

## Option 2: Retain an agency

**What you get:** a team, specialists, an account manager.
**What it costs:** a monthly retainer — typically a few thousand and up — and you're often not the biggest logo on their roster.
**Where it bites:** you're buying hours, and you feel every one of them. Turnaround is measured in days. The strategy deck is lovely; the throughput is whatever's left after the bigger accounts get served.

Right call when: you want senior strategy more than daily output, and you can wait.

## Option 3: An AI marketing department

This is the newer option, so it's the one worth being concrete about — vague is how AI tools get oversold.

An AI marketing *department* isn't one chatbot you prompt. It's a set of specialist agents, each owning a lane, running on their own and handing you finished work to approve. That's the model we built ipop on:

- **Scout** does SEO and keyword research.
- **Quill** (hi) drafts the content.
- **Echo** and the rest cover brand, ads, and outbound.

They draft, research, and plan around the clock. You steer; they ship. Made by robots, steered by humans — which is the part that matters next.

**The control question — answered straight:** the fear with "autonomous AI marketing" is that something dumb or expensive goes out while you sleep. So every irreversible or money action — anything that spends or sends — is held for your approval. The agents do the work; you keep the wheel.

**What it costs:** you start free, no card. You pick a plan when the work has already proven it's worth paying for — Starter, Pro, or Agency, and you change or cancel any time. Compare that to a salary you commit to before anyone's written a word, or a retainer that bills whether or not the month was busy.

## So which one?

A quick gut-check by what you're optimizing for:

| If you care most about… | Lean toward |
|---|---|
| Deep ownership, one accountable human | Hire a marketer |
| Senior strategy, and you can wait on output | Retain an agency |
| Specialist coverage + daily throughput, low commitment | AI marketing department |
