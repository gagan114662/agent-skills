### What an "AI marketing agency for startups" actually does (and what it doesn't)

You're a founder. Marketing is the thing that keeps sliding to next week. You've got three real options on the table, and "AI marketing agency" is the new one you're not sure about. Let's lay them next to each other without the sales fog.

**Your three options, honestly:**

| | Hire someone | Traditional agency | AI marketing department |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time to first work | Weeks to hire | 2–4 week onboarding | Same day |
| Monthly cost | One salary, one skillset | Retainer, often $3k+ | Starts free, no card |
| Breadth | One person, one strength | A team, on their clock | SEO, brand, content, ads, outbound — at once |
| Who decides | They do | They pitch, you nod | **You approve everything that spends or sends** |

That last row is the one most "AI marketing" pitches skip, so we'll plant a flag on it: **nothing irreversible happens without you.** Every money move and every outbound send is held for human approval. The robots draft, research, and plan around the clock; you steer.

### What you actually get on day one

An AI marketing agency for a startup shouldn't be one chatbot wearing five hats. Ours is a department of specialists, each with a job:

- **Scout** runs SEO and research — finds the keywords worth chasing and the gaps competitors left open.
- **Quill** (that's the writer) drafts the posts, the pages, the emails.
- **Lens** keeps the brand consistent so everything sounds like *you*, not like a template.
- The rest cover ads and outbound.

They work in parallel, which is the part a single hire can't match. While Scout is auditing your site, Quill is drafting, and you're reviewing — not waiting in a queue.

### "But will it just publish weird stuff in my name?"

No — and this is the design, not a setting you have to remember to flip. Drafts stay drafts until a human says go. Spend stays paused until a human approves it. You get the speed of a tireless team with the brakes of a human in the chair. Made by robots, steered by humans.

### How to try it without a meeting

Here's the honest sales pitch: start free, no card. Let the agents do real work — an SEO audit, a content draft, a campaign plan — and see if it's worth anything before you pay a cent. Pick a plan when the work pays for itself, and change or cancel any time.

That's the whole bet. If the work's good, you'll know. If it isn't, you've spent nothing but a few minutes steering.
