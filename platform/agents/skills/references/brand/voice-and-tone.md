# Voice and Tone

**Voice is who we are. Tone is how we sound right now.** Voice never changes — it is the
personality behind every word ipop ships. Tone bends to context: the same voice is calm in
an outage note, celebratory in a launch tweet, careful in a billing email. If you find
yourself rewriting *who we are* to fit a channel, you've confused the two. Flex tone, never voice.

## Write the voice as dimensions, not adjectives

"Friendly and professional" is useless — every brand claims it. Define voice as 3–4
**dimensions**, each a spectrum with a chosen position and a do/don't pair. ipop's voice:

**1. Warm, not chummy.** We talk like a competent friend, not a mascot.
- Do: "We'll handle the busywork so you can get back to the actual work."
- Don't: "Hey besties!! 🎉 Let's gooo crush some marketing today!!!"

**2. First-person plural, always.** ipop is a team of agents working *with* you. "We," never "the platform" or "the system."
- Do: "We drafted three subject lines and shipped the one that tested best."
- Don't: "The system has generated subject line variants for user review."

**3. Receipts over adjectives.** We don't say "powerful" or "seamless." We show the number.
- Do: "Open rate went 18% → 31% after we rewrote the preview text."
- Don't: "Our powerful AI dramatically boosts your email performance."

**4. A little playful, never goofy.** One wink per page, earned, then we get back to work.
- Do: "Made by robots, steered by humans."
- Don't: "Our AI hamsters are spinning up your campaign! 🐹"

A new writer should be able to read these four lines and produce on-voice copy without asking.

## The "would a real person say this?" test

Read the sentence aloud. If a smart colleague wouldn't say it across a desk, cut it.
"Leverage synergies to optimize your funnel" fails instantly. "We found where people drop
off and fixed it" passes. This single test kills 80% of bad copy.

## Kill corporate-speak and AI-slop

Two failure modes, both lethal to a brand voice.

**Corporate-speak** — empty abstraction: *leverage, utilize, robust, seamless, best-in-class,
solutions, empower, unlock, streamline, cutting-edge.* Banned. Replace with the plain verb:
*utilize → use, leverage → use, empower you to → let you.*

**AI-slop** — the tells of unedited model output: "In today's fast-paced world," "It's
important to note," "Let's dive in," "elevate your brand," tidy rule-of-three lists for
everything, the em-dash-and-restate rhythm, and relentless cheerful hedging. Slop is
*grammatically perfect and says nothing.* The fix is specificity: name the real number, the
real customer, the real result. Vague + polished = slop; specific + plain = voice.

## Apply the same voice across surfaces — flex only tone

Voice is identical everywhere; tone shifts:

- **Homepage** (confident, declarative): "We run your marketing. You approve the spend. That's it."
- **Email** (warm, direct, one ask): "We shipped this week's newsletter — 2,400 opens so far. Want us to run the follow-up?"
- **Social** (playful, short, a number): "Rewrote one subject line. 18% → 31%. Made by robots, steered by humans."
- **Error / billing** (calm, plain, owns it): "We paused the campaign — the ad budget hit your cap. Approve more spend and we'll resume in a minute."

Same team, same plain warmth, same receipts. Only the volume and pacing move. When in doubt,
read it aloud, check it against the four dimensions, and cut anything a real person wouldn't say.
