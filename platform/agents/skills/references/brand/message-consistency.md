# Message Consistency

A brand is a single story told a thousand times without drift. The job is not to write more
messages — it's to write *one* and protect it everywhere.

## The positioning statement (the master source)

Everything downstream derives from one sentence. Use the classic template:

> **For** [who] **who** [need], **we are the** [category] **that** [differentiator], **unlike** [alternative].

ipop's positioning, worked:

> **For** founders and small teams **who** can't afford a marketing department, **ipop is the**
> autonomous marketing team **that** actually runs your campaigns end-to-end — drafts, ships,
> measures, repeats — while you just approve the spend, **unlike** AI writing tools that hand
> you a draft and leave the work to you.

Note what each slot does: **who** is narrow (not "everyone"), **need** is a real pain (not "wants
better marketing"), **category** plants a flag ("autonomous marketing team," not "tool"),
**differentiator** is the one thing only we do, **unlike** names the status quo we beat. If you
can't fill every slot with something specific, the positioning isn't ready — and no amount of
clever copy downstream will rescue a vague core.

## Differentiation over description

Describing what you do is table stakes; everyone in the category describes the same things.
Lead with what makes you *different*, not what makes you *competent*.

- Description (weak): "ipop writes emails, posts, and ads with AI."
- Differentiation (strong): "ipop doesn't hand you a draft — it ships the campaign and shows you the numbers."

Competitors say "AI-powered content." We say "you approve the spend, we do the rest." The
differentiator is the autonomy + the human approval gate. Every headline should reach for that,
not for the feature list.

## Message hierarchy: one primary, three proofs

Pick **one** primary message. Support it with **three** proof points, each a fact, not an
adjective. That's the whole hierarchy.

- **Primary:** "Your marketing runs itself. You just approve the spend."
- **Proof 1:** End-to-end — drafts, ships, and measures every channel (not just generates text).
- **Proof 2:** You stay in control — every dollar passes the Approval Queue before it's spent.
- **Proof 3:** Receipts — "18% → 31% open rate," real numbers from real sends.

Three proofs, no more. A fourth dilutes; a missing proof leaves a claim unbacked. Each proof is
checkable. The hierarchy means a homepage hero, a sales email, and an investor line all rank the
*same* primary message first — they differ only in how much proof they have room for.

## The "so what?" test

Run every line through it. After each sentence, ask "so what?" — if there's no answer that
matters to the reader, cut or sharpen the line.

- "ipop uses advanced AI." → *So what?* → It runs your campaigns without you. **(keep the second one.)**
- "We support email, social, and ads." → *So what?* → One team covers every channel, so you don't hire three. **(rewrite to the payoff.)**
- "Built by a world-class team." → *So what?* → ...nothing the reader can use. **(cut.)**

The test forces every line down to reader payoff. Features that survive get rewritten as
benefits; features that don't get deleted.

## Keep the story identical from tweet to homepage

Same story, different length — never a different story. All three below ladder up to the same
primary message and the same differentiator:

- **Tweet (1 line):** "Your marketing runs itself. You approve the spend. Made by robots, steered by humans."
- **Email subject + line:** "We ran your marketing this week — here's what shipped." / "Three campaigns out, 2,400 opens, $40 spent against your cap. Approve next week's plan?"
- **Homepage hero:** "ipop is your autonomous marketing team. It drafts, ships, and measures every campaign — you just approve the spend."

If a reader saw all three, they should feel one brand, not three. To guarantee that: write the
positioning statement first, derive the message hierarchy from it, and check every surface back
against both before it ships. When a new channel tempts you to invent a fresh angle, resist —
shorten the existing story instead. Consistency *is* the brand.
