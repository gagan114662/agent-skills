# Editing Rubric

A B draft is competent and forgettable. An A draft is specific and unskippable. The gap is almost never "more writing" — it's subtraction and proof. Run this checklist on every draft, in order. Don't trust your read; run the tests.

## Pass 1 — Cut every word not doing work

- **Adjective audit.** For each adjective, ask "does this carry information?" "A powerful, robust, seamless solution" → delete all three; they're filler. Keep adjectives that change the meaning ("a *7-line* config," "a *read-only* token").
- **Adverb purge.** "Very," "really," "quite," "actually," "basically" — delete on sight. "Significantly faster" → give the number.
- **Qualifier kill.** "I think," "it seems," "arguably," "in some cases," "to a degree." Hedging signals you don't believe your own claim. State it or cut it.
- **Wind-up phrases.** "It's worth noting that," "It's important to remember," "At the end of the day." Start the sentence at the noun.

Target: 15-25% shorter after Pass 1. If you cut nothing, you didn't edit.

## Pass 2 — Replace every claim with a receipt

For each assertion, demand one of: a **number**, a **named example**, or a **source**.

- "Cold email is dead" → "Reply rates on cold email fell from 8% to 1.7% (2019→2024, Belkins data)."
- "This approach scales" → "We ran it on 40k rows; p95 stayed under 200ms."
- "Customers love it" → name the customer and the quote.

If a claim has no receipt available, either find one or delete the claim. Unsupported assertions are the #1 marker of a B draft.

## Pass 3 — The "could this open any article in the niche?" test

Read your opening paragraph. Ask: **could this exact intro sit atop any competitor's post on this topic?** If yes, it's generic — rewrite until it could only introduce *your* piece (your angle, your number, your specific reader). The same test applies to every section's first sentence.

## Pass 4 — Skimmability

- **Paragraphs ≤ 3-4 sentences.** Walls of text don't get read on screens. Break them.
- **Subheads tell the story alone.** Read only your H2s top to bottom — do they form the argument? If a subhead is a label ("Background," "Overview," "Conclusion"), rewrite it as a claim.
- **One idea per paragraph.** First sentence states it; the rest proves it.
- **Front-load.** Put the conclusion first in each section, support after. Inverted pyramid.

## Pass 5 — One CTA

Count your asks. If you ask the reader to subscribe, follow, buy, *and* download, you've asked for nothing. Pick the one action that matches the reader's stage; cut or demote the rest.

## Pass 6 — Kill AI-slop and clichés

Search-and-destroy these exact phrases (and their cousins):

- "In today's fast-paced world / digital landscape / ever-evolving"
- "Delve," "tapestry," "testament to," "navigate the complexities," "unlock," "unleash," "elevate," "robust," "seamless," "leverage" (as a verb when "use" works)
- "It's not just X, it's Y" / "Whether you're a X or a Y"
- "In conclusion," "In summary," "At the end of the day"
- Emoji-bulleted listicles with no substance; rule-of-three padding.

These signal machine-generated or lazy writing and instantly lose a discerning reader.

## Pass 7 — Read it aloud

Actually read the whole draft out loud (or have TTS read it). The ear catches what the eye forgives:

- Sentences you run out of breath on → split them.
- Rhythm that thuds (every sentence the same length) → vary it; follow a long sentence with a short one.
- Words you stumble on → simpler word.
- Spots where you'd naturally pause or add "you know what I mean" → the writing is unclear there; fix it.

## Ship gate

Before publishing, confirm: cut ≥15%, every claim has a receipt, opening is non-generic, subheads tell the story, exactly one CTA, zero slop phrases, passed read-aloud. If any fails, it's still a B.

made by robots, steered by humans.
