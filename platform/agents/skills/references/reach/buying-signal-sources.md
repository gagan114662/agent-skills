# Buying Signal Sources

Cold outreach is unwelcome because it arrives with no reason and no timing. A **buying signal** supplies both: a public event that proves a prospect's pain just got sharper, and a natural reason to reach out *now*. Signal-led outreach feels human because it *is* human — you noticed something real and responded to it.

## What counts as a buying signal

A buying signal is an observable event that makes your ICP's pain acute. The strongest ones map directly to a problem you solve:

- **Funding round** — new budget and a growth mandate. For ipop's ICP, a seed raise is the canonical "now they need a marketing engine" trigger.
- **Job change / new hire** — a founder hiring a "head of growth," or a champion moving to a new company who already knows your value.
- **Hiring for a role you replace** — a startup posting a "growth marketer" job *is the pain made public*: they have the need and no one to fill it yet.
- **Product launch** — they just shipped and now need distribution they don't have.
- **A relevant public post** — a founder tweeting "we have users but I have no idea how to do marketing" is a stated problem, in their own words.
- **Expansion signals** — new market entry, a pricing change, a new feature line — each implies a growth push.
- **Competitor / category moves** — a rival's launch or raise that puts pressure on them.

The best signals are **recent** (days, not months), **specific to the person**, and **causally connected** to what you offer.

## Where to find them — ethically

Use public, professional sources. Never scrape private data, never buy shady lists, never use credentials you weren't given.

- **Funding databases & newsletters** — Crunchbase, public raise announcements, and curated funding newsletters surface just-funded companies daily.
- **Job boards & careers pages** — a live "growth marketer" req is a free, explicit signal. Check company careers pages, LinkedIn Jobs, YC's job board, AngelList.
- **LinkedIn** — job changes, new-hire announcements, and founder posts. Following people and reading public posts is fine; respect platform terms and don't automate scraping.
- **Product launch platforms** — Product Hunt, Hacker News "Show HN," and indie maker communities show who just shipped.
- **Founder communities & social** — public X/Twitter, indie hacker forums, and Slack/Discord communities where founders openly describe their problems.
- **Press & changelogs** — TechCrunch-style coverage, company blogs, and public changelogs reveal expansion and launches.
- **Your own first-party signals** — site visits, content downloads, demo views, and inbound interactions are the warmest signals of all and fully consented.

## Turning a signal into timely, human outreach

A signal is wasted if the message doesn't reference it specifically. The pattern:

1. **Open with the observed event** — show you actually noticed. "Saw you just closed your seed round — congrats."
2. **Connect it to the pain** — make the relevance explicit. "Round usually means a growth number lands on the founder before there's anyone to own it."
3. **Offer a specific, small next step** — proportional to the moment, not a hard pitch.

**Worked example (ipop):** A founder posts on X: *"We hit 500 users on our launch but I'm a solo technical founder and have no clue how to do marketing."* That's pain in their own words. Good outbound: *"Congrats on 500 from launch — that's real traction. The 'technical founder with no marketing function' gap is exactly the spot we built ipop for: it runs the marketing a team would, without the hire. Happy to show what your first month of demand-gen could look like if useful."* Timely (post-launch), specific (their words), relevant (their stated problem), low-pressure.

**Anti-pattern:** Pulling a name from a funded list and sending "Hi {first_name}, we help startups grow, got 15 min?" — same list, zero signal, instant delete.

## The discipline

- **No signal, no send.** If you can't name the specific reason this person, this week — wait or skip.
- **Recency matters** — a six-month-old funding round is not a trigger.
- **One signal, one tailored message** — never paste the same line under a different event.

Signals are how outbound earns the right to interrupt. Use real ones, reference them honestly, and the cold email stops being cold.
