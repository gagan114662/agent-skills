# ICP Playbook

A vague ICP ("startups," "SMBs," "marketers") produces vague outreach that gets ignored. A tight ICP is the single highest-leverage decision in outbound: it determines who you reach, what pain you lead with, and whether the message feels written *for them* or blasted *at them*. Get this right and every downstream step — list, message, timing, offer — improves for free.

## The three-part ICP

A real ICP is not just firmographics. It is firmographics **plus a specific pain plus a trigger**.

1. **Firmographics (the container).** The observable, filterable attributes: company stage, team size, industry, business model, geography, tech stack, funding. These are how you *find* accounts.
2. **The specific pain (the reason).** The concrete problem this profile has that your product removes. Not "they want growth" — that's everyone. Name the exact friction: "they know they need marketing but can't justify a $120k/yr hire and don't have time to do it themselves."
3. **The trigger (the timing).** The event that makes the pain *acute right now*: a funding round, a product launch, a new competitor, a leadership hire, a missed quarter. Pain without a trigger is a someday-problem; a trigger turns it into a this-week problem.

The pain is what your message leads with. The trigger is what makes the message timely. The firmographics are just how you build the list.

## The "who feels this pain most acutely right now" filter

Most teams pick the *largest* addressable segment. Pick the *most acute* one instead. Rank candidate segments on three axes:

- **Pain intensity** — how much does this hurt? (annoyance vs. blocking a goal)
- **Pain frequency** — how often does it bite? (quarterly vs. daily)
- **Buying readiness** — can they act now? (budget + authority + a trigger event)

Your beachhead ICP is the segment that scores highest on all three — the people for whom the pain is sharp, recurring, and currently un-solved. They convert faster, churn less, and refer others like them. Win the acute segment first, then expand outward.

## Segmentation

Once you have a beachhead, segment *within* it so messaging stays specific:

- **By trigger** — funded-this-quarter vs. just-launched vs. just-hired-a-founder-marketer. Each gets a different opening line.
- **By role** — the founder feels the pain; a future ops hire feels it differently. Message the person who owns the problem today.
- **By context** — solo founder vs. two-person team changes what "can't hire" means.

One segment, one trigger, one pain, one message. That is the unit of good outbound.

## Worked example: ipop's ICP

- **Firmographics:** Pre-seed to seed startups, 1–10 people, founder-led, no marketing hire yet, B2B or prosumer SaaS, post-launch (has a live product/site), often technical founders.
- **Specific pain:** "We have a product and early users but no marketing engine. We can't justify a full-time marketer or an agency retainer, and the founders are buried in product. Growth is stalling because nobody owns demand."
- **Trigger:** Just closed a round (now has budget + a growth mandate), OR just launched publicly (now needs distribution), OR just got a board nudge on pipeline.
- **Most-acute filter:** A *just-funded, just-launched, technical-founder* team scores highest — sharp pain, immediate readiness, clear trigger. That's the beachhead.

Notice how much the message writes itself once the ICP is this tight: lead with "you just raised and now own a growth number you don't have a team for," not "we help startups grow."

## Verification

A tight ICP passes these checks:
- You can name a real company that fits it perfectly.
- The pain statement would make that founder nod, not shrug.
- There is an observable trigger you could actually find this week.
- A stranger reading your ICP could build the same list you would.

If any check fails, the ICP is still too loose — narrow it.
