# Cold Email Compliance & Ethics

Outbound done right is permission-aware, relevant, and honest. Done wrong it is illegal, damages your domain forever, and erodes trust. The rules below are the floor; the ethical bar sits above them.

## CAN-SPAM (US) — the hard rules

CAN-SPAM permits cold B2B email but mandates specific conduct. Violations carry per-email penalties.

1. **Accurate headers and "From."** The From, Reply-To, and routing must identify the real sender. No spoofing, no fake domains, no impersonation.
2. **Non-deceptive subject lines.** The subject must reflect the actual content. No "Re:" on a first-touch email, no fake urgency, no bait.
3. **Identify the message as outreach** if its nature isn't obvious, and be clear who you are.
4. **Include a valid physical postal address** for your business in every email.
5. **Provide a clear, working opt-out** mechanism (a reply-to-unsubscribe or link is fine for B2B).
6. **Honor opt-outs promptly** — within 10 business days, and keep them off your lists permanently. Never sell or transfer a suppressed address.

## GDPR (EU/UK) — the higher bar

When you email people in the EU/UK, GDPR governs the personal data you process (a name + work email is personal data).

- **Lawful basis.** For B2B cold email the usual basis is **legitimate interest**, but you must (a) document a legitimate-interest assessment, (b) ensure your offer is genuinely relevant to that person's professional role, and (c) keep the intrusion proportionate. Consumer/personal addresses generally require **consent** — don't cold-email them.
- **Transparency.** Tell recipients who you are, why you're contacting them, where you got their data, and how to object — typically via a privacy-policy link.
- **Right to object / erasure.** Honor unsubscribe and deletion requests immediately and permanently.
- **Data minimization.** Hold only what you need (name, work email, company, role). Don't hoard scraped personal data.
- **Source hygiene.** Don't buy sketchy lists. Use data obtained from legitimate, professional sources.

## The line between legitimate B2B outreach and spam

| Legitimate outreach | Spam |
|---|---|
| Targeted to a real ICP and role | Blasted to anyone with an email |
| References a real, relevant reason to reach out | Generic "Hi {first_name}, can I get 15 min?" |
| Honest identity, subject, and offer | Deceptive headers, fake "Re:", false urgency |
| Easy, honored opt-out | Hidden, broken, or ignored unsubscribe |
| Low volume, high relevance | High volume, hope-something-sticks |

The test: *Would this person, reading it, reasonably feel you did your homework and respected their time?* If not, don't send it.

## Why permission + relevance beat volume

Volume is a trap. Mass-blasting torches your reputation faster than it generates replies. Relevant outreach to 50 well-chosen people outperforms 5,000 cold-blasted ones on reply rate, on conversion, and on the one thing volume can't buy back: your sender reputation.

## Deliverability protection (this is also an ethics signal)

Mailbox providers reward senders who behave and quarantine those who don't.

- **Authenticate** with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC on your sending domain.
- **Warm up** new domains/inboxes gradually; don't go zero-to-thousands.
- **Use a separate sending domain** from your primary brand domain to isolate risk.
- **Verify addresses** before sending; bounces and spam-traps wreck your score.
- **Watch complaint and bounce rates** — high rates are the system telling you your targeting is wrong.
- **Cap volume per inbox** and keep copy human; bulk-templated patterns get filtered.

## The ethical bar (non-negotiable)

- **Never deceptive** — not in identity, subject, claims, or "personalization" that's actually a mail-merge.
- **Never mass-blast** — relevance over reach, always.
- **Never email people who said no** — opt-out is permanent and absolute.
- **Never fabricate** familiarity, urgency, or social proof.
- **Default to respect** — if a send wouldn't survive the recipient forwarding it to a peer with "look at this spam," don't send it.

Compliance keeps you legal. The ethical bar keeps you welcome. Aim for welcome.
