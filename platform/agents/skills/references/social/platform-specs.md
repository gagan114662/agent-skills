# Platform Specs — X/Twitter & LinkedIn

The two ipop targets reward opposite behaviors. X rewards velocity and reply-bait; LinkedIn rewards dwell time and saves. Writing the same post for both wastes the strengths of each.

## X / Twitter

**What the algorithm rewards (in weight order):** replies > reposts > likes, plus *dwell time* (how long the post is on screen) and profile clicks. Replies are weighted heaviest by far — a post that sparks conversation outranks one that just gets liked. Outbound links are suppressed; put links in a reply, not the post.

**Single post sweet spot:** 70–100 characters for max reach; the full 280 is fine for a complete thought. Verified accounts can post long-form (up to 25k chars) — long posts can win when they read as a self-contained essay, but the first 280 still must hook.

**Threads:** use when the idea genuinely needs sequence (a story, a numbered method, a teardown). Each tweet must stand alone *and* pull to the next. Hook tweet does 90% of the work — it decides whether anyone reads tweet 2. Aim 5–9 tweets; longer threads bleed readers per step. End with a CTA (follow / the one link / "bookmark this").

**Cadence:** 1–4 posts/day for an active account. Consistency over volume. Reply to others 5–10x for every post you publish — replies build reach faster than posts for accounts under ~10k followers.

**Hashtags:** essentially dead for reach on X. Use 0. A hashtag can look spammy and slightly suppress. Topic discovery happens via keywords and the For You graph, not tags.

**Media:** images 16:9 (1600×900) or square (1080×1080); native video 1080×1920 vertical or 1280×720 landscape, under 2:20 ideally, autoplays muted so the first frame must work without sound. Posts with native media get more dwell; links to external media get less reach than uploaded media.

## LinkedIn

**What the algorithm rewards:** dwell time and *saves/shares* over likes, plus early comments (especially substantive ones, not "Great post!"). The "golden hour" matters — engagement in the first 60–90 minutes sets distribution. Outbound links in the post body suppress reach; put the link in the first comment and say "link below."

**The first-two-lines rule:** LinkedIn truncates at ~140–210 characters behind "...see more." Those two lines are your entire ad for the post. Lead with the hook, never with context. Put a line break before the "see more" cut so the expand button sits on its own line — it reads cleaner and lifts expand rate.

**Post structure that works:** short hook (1–2 lines) → line break → one idea per line with whitespace between → a payoff or list → a single question CTA. Whitespace is a feature on LinkedIn; dense paragraphs lose mobile readers. Sweet spot 1,200–1,600 characters (max 3,000).

**Single vs. carousel/document:** text posts are the default. Document/carousel posts (PDF uploads, 1080×1080 or 1080×1350 pages, 5–10 pages) get strong dwell and saves when the content is genuinely skimmable and useful. Native video 1080×1920, captioned (most watch muted).

**Cadence:** 3–5 posts/week is the durable rhythm. Daily is fine if quality holds; quality collapse hurts more than a skipped day. Space posts ≥18–24h apart so they don't compete.

**Hashtags:** 3–5 relevant ones, placed at the end. They help categorization modestly — not a growth lever, but not dead like on X. Avoid stuffing.

**Comments are content.** Reply to every comment in the first hour; each reply is a new engagement event that re-surfaces the post. Ask the commenter a follow-up question to extend the thread.
