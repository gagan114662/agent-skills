# Hook Library

The hook is the only line guaranteed to be read. On X it's what shows before "Show more"; on LinkedIn it's the two lines before "...see more". If it doesn't create tension, the rest of the post never gets seen. Write the hook last, after you know your payoff — then make the hook promise exactly that payoff.

## The patterns

**1. The curiosity gap** — open a loop the reader must close.
> "The best-performing post we ever ran broke every rule I'd been taught."
Works when you have a genuine non-obvious resolution. Fails if the payoff is mundane — a betrayed gap reads as clickbait and kills trust for the next post.

**2. The contrarian take** — attack a consensus belief the audience holds.
> "Posting daily is the worst advice in marketing."
Works when your audience has internalized the "rule" you're breaking and you can defend the reversal with evidence. Needs a real argument underneath, not just provocation.

**3. The specific number** — concreteness signals it actually happened.
> "We cut our CAC from $340 to $90 in 11 weeks. Here's the exact sequence."
Works almost always — odd, precise numbers ($340, not "around $300"; 11 weeks, not "a few months") read as data, not marketing. The specificity *is* the credibility.

**4. The callout** — name the exact person who needs this.
> "If you're a founder doing your own marketing and it's not working: this is why."
Works when you can name a narrow, self-identifying segment. The reader thinks "that's me" and stops scrolling. Broader the callout, weaker the stop.

**5. The story-open** — drop into a scene mid-action, no setup.
> "Three days before launch, our biggest customer asked for a refund."
Works for posts carrying a lesson — humans are wired to finish stories. The trick is starting at the moment of tension, not "Last year I decided to..."

**6. The "I was wrong"** — admit a costly mistake.
> "I spent $40k on ads before I realized I was measuring the wrong thing."
Works because vulnerability + a stake (money, time, reputation) buys attention cheaply and makes the lesson land. Only use real mistakes; manufactured humility is transparent.

**7. The before/after** — show transformation in one line.
> "18 months ago: 200 followers, zero leads. Today: 40k followers, 60% of pipeline. What changed:"
Works when the delta is large and the timeframe is named. Implicitly promises a repeatable method — so the body must deliver one.

## Rules of a great hook

- **Specificity beats cleverness.** "$340 to $90" outperforms "we slashed costs dramatically" every time. Numbers, names, dates, dollar amounts.
- **Front-load tension.** A question the reader can't answer, a claim they want to dispute, a gap they need closed. No tension, no stop.
- **No throat-clearing.** Delete "Excited to share," "I've been thinking about," "Quick thread on," "So I wanted to talk about." These spend your one guaranteed line on nothing. Start at the sharpest word.
- **One idea per hook.** Two competing claims dilute both. Pick the sharpest, lead with it.
- **The hook is a promise — keep it.** The body must pay off exactly what the hook set up. Over-promise once and the audience discounts you forever.
- **Cut the first line, then re-read.** Most drafts' real hook is sentence two. Sentence one was the warm-up.
