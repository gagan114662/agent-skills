# Subject Line Rubric

The subject line is ~80% of whether the email is read at all. Nothing else in the email matters if it isn't opened. Write the subject *last*, after you know the one promise the email pays off — then make sure the body cashes the check the subject writes.

## Patterns that work (with examples)

- **Specific-value** — name the concrete outcome. "Cut your invoice time from 40 min to 4." > "Save time on invoices." Numbers and nouns beat adjectives.
- **Curiosity-gap** — open a loop the body closes. "The pricing mistake almost everyone makes." Only works if the body *delivers* the answer; an unpaid loop trains people to stop opening.
- **Personal / plain** — looks like it came from a human, not a campaign. "quick question, Sara" / "re: your account". Often out-performs designed subjects because it reads like a 1:1 note. Use sparingly and never deceptively (no fake "re:" if there was no thread).
- **Question** — engages the reader's own situation. "Still exporting to spreadsheets by hand?" Best when the answer is "yes, ugh."
- **Number / list** — sets a clear, finite expectation. "3 settings to change before you send." Odd numbers and specific figures ("$1,284 saved") feel more credible than round ones.

## What kills open rates (and reputation)

- **Clickbait you can't pay off.** "You won't believe this..." then a dull body = the fastest way to train unopens and earn spam complaints. The subject is a promise; break it and the next 10 sends suffer.
- **Spam-trigger words / patterns.** FREE, "act now", "100% guaranteed", "$$$", "winner", "this isn't spam", excessive exclamation. Filters and humans both flag them.
- **ALL CAPS** and **emoji spam** (🔥🔥💰) — reads as shouting; lowers opens and raises complaints.
- **Too long** — anything past ~7 words / ~40 chars gets truncated on mobile (most opens are mobile). The hook must land in the first ~30 characters.
- **Vague nothing-subjects** — "Newsletter #42", "An update from us." Zero reason to open.
- **Misleading "personalization"** — broken merge tags ("Hi {FIRST_NAME}") are worse than no name.

## The preview text is a second subject line

The preview (preheader) is the gray text the inbox shows after the subject. Most senders waste it on "View in browser" or "If you can't see images...". Instead, write it as a deliberate *continuation* of the subject — extend the hook, don't repeat it.

- Subject: "Your trial ends tomorrow" → Preview: "Here's the one-click way to keep your data."
- Set ~40–90 chars; if you leave it blank the client scrapes the first body line, which is usually ugly.

## Ideal length

Aim for **~6–8 words, 30–50 characters**. The decisive part is the first ~30 chars (mobile truncation). Shorter, concrete subjects generally beat longer clever ones.

## A/B testing approach

1. **Test one variable.** Change *only* the subject (or only the preview) — never both, or you can't attribute the lift.
2. **Form a hypothesis.** "Curiosity beats specific-value for our re-engagement list."
3. **Size the split.** Send A and B to a random 10–20% each; auto-send the winner to the remaining 60–80% after a 2–4h read window.
4. **Pick the right metric.** Optimize for the *downstream* goal (clicks, replies, conversions), not opens alone — Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates opens and makes open-rate a noisy signal. Use opens directionally; trust clicks/conversions.
5. **Need real significance.** A 0.3% open delta on 500 recipients is noise. Require a meaningful gap and adequate sample, or run it again.
6. **Log every result.** Build a house style guide of what wins *for your audience* — borrowed best-practices are a starting hypothesis, not an answer.
