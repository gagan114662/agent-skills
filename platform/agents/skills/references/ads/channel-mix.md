# Channel Mix

The first question is never "which channel?" — it's **"am I capturing existing demand or creating new demand?"** Search captures; paid social creates; retargeting closes. Picking the wrong primitive for your stage wastes the whole budget, no matter how good the creative.

## Search — capture existing high-intent demand

Someone typing "marketing automation for agencies" has already decided they have the problem; you're competing to be the answer. Highest intent, highest conversion rate, **highest CPC** because everyone bids on the same words.

- **Fits when:** there is existing search volume for your category. Verify in Keyword Planner — if your head terms get <100–500 searches/month, the demand isn't there yet and search can't manufacture it.
- **Structure:** tight ad groups, 1 theme each; start with **phrase + exact match**, never broad on a new account (broad needs conversion data to steer and will torch budget first). Mine the search-terms report weekly; add negatives aggressively.
- **Message match:** the keyword, headline, and landing-page H1 must echo the *same phrase*. Searcher types "HIPAA-compliant scheduling" → headline says HIPAA-compliant scheduling → page proves it. Every mismatch leaks Quality Score (raising CPC) and conversion rate.
- **Early B2B SaaS:** start here if category demand exists. Cheapest path to your first design-partner conversations because intent is pre-qualified. Expect $8–25 CPC in competitive B2B verticals; one demo from 30 clicks can still pencil at a $5k ACV.

## Paid social — create demand by interruption

On Meta/LinkedIn/TikTok nobody is searching for you; you interrupt a scroll. You must *create* the problem-awareness, so creative carries 80% of the outcome. Lower intent, but vastly larger reach and the only way to grow a category that has no search volume yet.

- **Fits when:** your buyer is identifiable by attribute (job title, interest, lookalike) but isn't actively searching — i.e., you're educating the market or your wedge is too new to have search demand.
- **LinkedIn** for B2B precision targeting (title/company/seniority) — expensive ($8–15 CPM, $50–200+ CPL) but the cleanest B2B audience. **Meta** for cheaper reach and lookalikes once you have a seed customer list.
- **Message match:** intent is *low*, so creative must do the work search keywords do elsewhere — hook on the *pain/outcome* in the first 3 seconds, not features. Native-feeling, not banner-y. The ad's job is to manufacture the click-intent the searcher already had.

## Retargeting — close warmed demand

Re-show ads to people who visited but didn't convert. Tiny audience, but the **highest ROAS in the account** because these people already showed intent. It doesn't create demand — it harvests what the other two channels warmed.

- **Fits when:** you already have traffic to retarget (so it's never your *first* channel). Segment by depth: pricing-page visitors get a different, harder-closing message than blog readers.
- **Cap frequency** (~3–5 impressions/week) — past that you fatigue the audience and brand-damage. Keep retargeting ≤15–20% of total spend; the audience is small and over-funding just raises frequency.

## How to start: one channel, prove it, then expand

Do **not** spread a thin budget across three channels — you'll under-fund each below its learning threshold and learn nothing from all of them.

1. **Pick one channel that matches your demand reality.** Search volume exists → start search. No search volume, identifiable buyer → start paid social.
2. **Prove it** to a stable CPA at or below target-CAC (see budget-pacing's learning-budget threshold).
3. **Add retargeting second** — it's nearly free ROAS once you have traffic.
4. **Add the second cold channel third**, only after the first is a proven, profitable engine.

Sequence beats spread. One working channel funds the next.
